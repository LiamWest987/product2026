Research & Validation
Objective
Use this worksheet to explore your users, identify opportunities, and turn early research into clear insights. Your goal is not to have all the answers yet. Focus on asking good questions, identifying patterns, and exploring what users may need or want.
1. Surveying
What are we looking for?
Think about who you would want to talk to in order to better understand the problem. Consider different types of users, their experiences, behaviors, frustrations, and expectations.
The goal is to learn from users rather than assume what users want.
Brainstorm
Who should we interview?
College students who own at least one Apple device
Students who spend time with friends in group settings
Students who share living spaces
What different types of users should we include?
Students with one vs several Apple devices
Students who are introverted vs extroverted
What do we want to learn from these people?
How shared moments are coordinated today, and how much of that coordination is manual
Any issues that they have experienced when trying to interact and coordinate with different types of people
What patterns or differences might we expect to find?
Introverted people might feel like establishing connections is less of a necessity
Extroverted people will lean towards being supportive 
Example
If your team is exploring a technology feature, you might survey:
Someone who uses the feature frequently
Someone who rarely uses it
Someone who uses a competing product
Someone who does not use the feature at all
You might ask what they like, what frustrates them, what they wish worked differently, and what would make the experience more useful.

4. User Personas Developed
What are we looking for?
Create a few representative users based on your research. Personas should describe real patterns you discover, rather than fictional characters created from assumptions.
Think about differences in experience, needs, behaviors, goals, and frustrations.
Persona 1
Name / Identifier: The Social Coordinator (Maya)
Age / Age Range: 18-21, undergraduate
Background: Undergrad who lives off campus with roommates and hangs out with friends every week (apartment hangouts, movies, coffee, parties, studying), almost all of their friends use iPhones
Technology Experience: Knows most iPhone features (4-5 out of 5) and owns 3 or more Apple devices
Goals
Get everyone together without a lot of back and forth
Keep everyone on the same page during the hangout
Make sure everyone gets the photos and pays their share
Behaviors
Plans everything in the group chat instead of a planning app
Shares location, AirDrops photos, plays music, and sends payments manually
Usually the one who starts plans and keeps the group on track
Needs
One place for the hangout that connects location, photos, music, and payments
Little to no setup for friends, joining should only take one tap
Sharing that turns off by itself when the hangout ends
Frustrations
Deciding what to do and dealing with people’s changing availability
Setting up the same things on everyone’s phone (location, photos, speaker)
Asking people when they’re arriving and splitting costs after every hangout
Persona 2
Name / Identifier: The Lowkey Joiner (Jordan)
Age / Age Range: 18-19, undergraduate
Background: Undergrad who lives at home or off campus without roommates, a little introverted (2-3 out of 5), and hangs out every couple of weeks (sports, games, watching movies or sports, school events)
Technology Experience: Knows their iPhone pretty well (4 out of 5) but keeps it simple, most friends use iPhones but not all of them
Goals
Hang out without having to be the organizer
Know who’s actually coming before committing
Keep money and plans simple
Behaviors
Only uses group chats to make plans, no planning apps
Joins plans instead of starting them
Handles music and payments manually
Needs
Simple features that still work when a friend doesn’t have an iPhone
Control over what gets shared and for how long
An easy way to see who’s coming and pay each other back
Frustrations
Getting a headcount and figuring out what the plan is
Splitting costs and handling group money
Group chats only kind of work (2-3 out of 5) and plans get lost
Example
One persona might be someone who is very comfortable exploring new technology and expects advanced features.
Another might be someone who values simplicity and only wants technology to work without requiring much setup.
Neither persona is necessarily "better." The purpose is to understand how different users may have different needs.

Questions to Consider

Example

Discover → Learn → Try → Use → Decide whether to continue
At each stage, consider what the user is doing, what they expect, and where the experience could be improved.

6. Success Metrics Defined
What are we looking for?
Decide how your team would know whether your idea, feature, or experience is successful.
Think beyond simply asking whether people "like" it. Consider what users actually do.
Brainstorm
What would success look like?
Friend groups use Spaces for most hangouts instead of setting up every phone by hand
What could we measure?
Spaces started per week and how many invited friends join
How many people start another Space within a month

Example
Instead of:
"Users like the feature."
A stronger metric might be:
"Users who try the feature return to use it regularly."
The exact metric will depend on what your team is trying to accomplish.

7. Research Insights
What are we looking for?
Review everything you learned from surveys, personas, and journey maps.
Look for patterns, unexpected findings, contradictions, and opportunities.
Your insights should help answer:
What did we learn about the user that we did not know before?
Key Insights
What we observed:
Everyone on the final form only uses group chats to plan, no planning apps
Everyone splits costs manually and struggles with deciding what to do
Changing availability was the biggest pain point in the earlier responses (4 of 6)
Most friend groups use iPhones but not all, one person owns no Apple devices
People are split on sharing their location, even for a short time
What this might mean:
The biggest opportunity is during the hangout, Spaces should connect location, photos, music, and payments
Spaces should be built into Messages with one tap to join since nobody uses planning apps
Sharing that turns off by itself and a way for non-iPhone friends to join are must haves
Only 10 responses so far, we need more before deciding on the MVP
